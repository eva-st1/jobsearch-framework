## How to use this reference

This reference records the basis for the skill as researched on 2026-08-12. Prefer primary platform documentation, official public guidance, and peer-reviewed research. Treat career-center guidance as practice advice, not causal proof. Re-check time-sensitive legal, platform, and market guidance before materially revising the skill.

## Supported principles

### Clear, structured writing matters

Wingate et al. followed 183 Canadian postsecondary co-op jobseekers. Greater résumé/cover-letter detail, clarity, and structure were associated with a higher interview share and shorter searches after controls. The observational setting and population limit causal and international generalization, so the skill uses this as support for rigorous writing—not a promised uplift.

Source: Timothy G. Wingate et al., “The Signals That Matter: Resumes, Cover Letters, and Success on the Job Search,” *International Journal of Selection and Assessment* 33(3), 2025. https://doi.org/10.1111/ijsa.70022

An online labor-marketplace field experiment found algorithmic writing assistance improved résumé writing and was associated with 8% more hires and 10% higher wages in that setting. The population and platform are narrower than a general professional labor market.

Source: Emma van Inwegen, Zanele T. Munyikwa, and John J. Horton, “Algorithmic Writing Assistance on Jobseekers' Resumes Increases Hires,” *Management Science*, 2025. https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2024.04528

### Errors create real screening risk

A scenario experiment with 445 recruiters found lower interview probabilities for résumés containing either a few or many spelling errors. The effect varied and should not be converted into a universal prediction, but it supports a strict proofreading gate.

Source: Philippe Sterkens et al., “Costly mistakes: Why and when spelling errors in resumes jeopardise interview chances,” *PLOS ONE* 18(4), 2023. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0283280

### Skills should be demonstrated and named truthfully

NACE's 2025 U.S. early-career employer survey found employers commonly sought evidence of problem-solving, teamwork, written communication, initiative, work ethic, and technical skills on résumés. Because the survey concerns U.S. early-career hiring, use it as evidence for demonstrating capabilities, not as a universal list or reason to add unsupported soft-skill labels.

Source: National Association of Colleges and Employers, *Job Outlook 2025*, revised January 2025. https://www.naceweb.org/docs/default-source/default-document-library/2025/publication/research-report/2025-nace-job-outlook-jan-2025.pdf

ESCO provides a current multilingual vocabulary connecting occupations with skills and qualifications. It can normalize terminology across English, Polish, and Norwegian and assist transparent job matching. ESCO associations are typical occupational relationships, not proof that a candidate has a skill.

Sources:

- European Commission, “About ESCO.” https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/about-esco
- European Commission, “Competence-based job matching.” https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/about-esco/escopedia/escopedia/competence-based-job-matching

### ATS compatibility is primarily a parsability problem

Greenhouse documents parsing failures caused by graphics, photos, image-only documents, tables, headers, footers, text boxes, columns, unclear sections, letter-spaced words, and abbreviated titles. Greenhouse supports common text document formats and currently recommends PDF to candidates in its Quick Apply product. This supports an ATS-safe primary layout and direct extraction tests.

Sources:

- Greenhouse, “Unsuccessful resume parse,” updated 2026-03-02. https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse
- Greenhouse, “Supported formats for resumes, cover letters and other candidate uploads,” updated 2025-10-27. https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/360052218132-Supported-formats-for-resumes-cover-letters-and-other-candidate-uploads
- Greenhouse, “Resume parsing with non-English languages,” updated 2026-05-06. https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/205019689-Resume-parsing-with-non-English-languages

No evidence found supports treating commercial ATS-match percentages as reliable predictions of an employer's screening decision. The method therefore uses a transparent requirement matrix and extraction tests instead.

### Tailoring is selection and clarification, not fabrication

Harvard's career guidance recommends a concise, fact-based, easy-to-scan résumé tailored to the target role and warns against generic AI authorship. Europass and NAV likewise recommend selecting relevant experience and tailoring the summary to the vacancy.

Sources:

- Harvard FAS Mignone Center, “Harvard College Guide to Creating a Strong Resume.” https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/
- Europass, “Create your Europass CV.” https://europass.europa.eu/en/create-europass-cv
- NAV/Arbeidsplassen, “Slik skriv du ein god CV.” https://arbeidsplassen.nav.no/slik-skriver-du-en-god-cv

## Market sources

### Poland

Current Polish data-protection authority guidance says ordinary recruitment data identified in the Labour Code does not require consent and calls the old practice of adding blanket consent to a CV inappropriate. It also notes that a photo is not required and that employers should not request excess data.

Source: Urząd Ochrony Danych Osobowych, “ABC rekrutacji,” published 2026-07-06. https://uodo.gov.pl/pl/701/4471

This current authority supersedes older job-office pages that still present a generic RODO clause as mandatory. Re-check the authority when legal treatment changes or an employer supplies specific wording.

### Norway

NAV recommends tailoring, reverse chronology, accurate language levels, relevant certificates/courses, volunteering, and a role-specific summary. EURES says Norwegian applications typically include a CV and cover letter and that employers often emphasize qualifications, teamwork, independence, and motivation.

Sources:

- NAV, “Søknaden og CV-en,” updated 2025-09-12. https://www.nav.no/soknaden-og-cv
- NAV/Arbeidsplassen, “Slik skriv du ein god CV.” https://arbeidsplassen.nav.no/slik-skriver-du-en-god-cv
- EURES, “Living and working conditions: Norway,” updated 2026-06-05. https://eures.europa.eu/living-and-working/living-and-working-conditions-europe/living-and-working-conditions-norway_en

### Switzerland

Swiss federal employment guidance describes the CV as the core of a complete application dossier, recommends clear reverse chronology, and allows up to three pages. It describes a photo and several personal details as customary possibilities. ETH guidance recommends two pages for bachelor/master profiles and up to three for doctoral candidates, while confirming Swiss-specific conventions differ from abroad.

Sources:

- arbeit.swiss, “Bewerbungstipps.” https://www.arbeit.swiss/de/stellensuchende/bewerbungstipps
- ETH Zurich Career Center, *Application Guide*, 2024 edition. https://ethz.ch/content/dam/ethz/main/industry/career-center/Bewerbungsratgeber/2024_BRG_EN.pdf
- ETH Zurich, “How to apply for a job.” https://ethz.ch/en/the-eth-zurich/working-teaching-and-research/welcome-center/job-search-and-application/how-to-apply-for-a-job.html

Because customary sensitive data is not automatically necessary, the skill distinguishes the documented local tradition from a privacy-minimizing default and requires explicit user choice.

### United Kingdom

The UK National Careers Service recommends contact details, a short introduction, reverse-chronological work history, education, and references without publishing a referee's contact details. It explicitly advises excluding age, date of birth, marital status, and nationality. GOV.UK recruitment guidance restricts questions about protected characteristics and says date of birth should be requested only where an age requirement is intrinsic to the job. Some public-sector processes require anonymized CVs, so employer instructions override the ordinary named-CV default.

Sources:

- National Careers Service, “How to write a CV.” https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections
- GOV.UK, “Employers: preventing discrimination—Recruitment.” https://www.gov.uk/employer-preventing-discrimination/recruitment
- GOV.UK, “Checking a job applicant's right to work.” https://www.gov.uk/check-job-applicant-right-to-work
- UK Civil Service Careers, “IPO Recruitment Support.” https://www.civil-service-careers.gov.uk/departments/working_for_ipo/ipo-recruitment-support/

### United States

The US Department of Labor-sponsored CareerOneStop guide recommends one or two pages, standard headings, a simple single-column layout, and avoiding tables, columns, images, graphics, and important information in headers or footers. EEOC guidance recommends limiting pre-employment information to job-relevant qualifications, says employers should not ask for an applicant photograph, and treats age, sex, national origin, religion, disability, and family-status inquiries as legally sensitive. Citizenship and employment-authorization verification generally occur through the I-9 process after an accepted offer, although application systems may ask narrow sponsorship or authorization questions.

USAJOBS is a distinct federal variant. Current federal guidance limits ordinary resumes to two pages and requires explicit employer/title, month/year dates, hours per week, and evidence addressing announcement qualifications. Some announcements may request a long-form clinical or research CV; follow the announcement.

Sources:

- CareerOneStop, “Resume Guide—Formatting.” https://www.careeronestop.org/JobSearch/Resumes/ResumeGuide/formatting.aspx
- U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, “Prohibited Employment Policies/Practices.” https://www.eeoc.gov/prohibited-employment-policiespractices
- U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, “Pre-Employment Inquiries and Citizenship.” https://www.eeoc.gov/pre-employment-inquiries-and-citizenship
- USAJOBS, “How do I write a resume for a federal job?” https://help.usajobs.gov/faq/application/documents/resume/what-to-include

### Europe and Europass

Europass supports reusable profiles and selective, tailored CV generation in 31 languages. Its own guidance recommends clear examples, vacancy alignment, reverse chronology, and concise selection rather than a full detailed history.

Source: European Commission, “Create your Europass CV.” https://europass.europa.eu/en/create-europass-cv

## AI-assisted recruiting and AI-skill evidence

Current recruiting products no longer justify a keyword-only model of ATS optimization. LinkedIn describes a 2026 Hiring Assistant stack that blends semantic retrieval, structured and Boolean signals, personalized recommendations, lookalike search, ranking, and LLM-based candidate evaluation. LinkedIn Recruiter may evaluate applicants using profile data, resumes, screening answers, and recruiter-defined qualifications. Greenhouse Talent Matching compares resumes against recruiter-defined and weighted skills, experience, titles, and optional industry criteria; it explicitly says that more synonymous skill terms do not necessarily produce a higher match category.

Sources, checked 2026-08-12:

- LinkedIn Engineering, “Semantic Search for AI Agents at Scale,” published 2026-06-11. https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/ai/semantic-search-for-ai-agents-at-scale-retrieval-and-ranking-for-linkedins-hiring-assistant
- LinkedIn Recruiter Help, “Review applicants with Hiring Assistant,” updated 2026-07. https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a7109476
- LinkedIn Recruiter Help, “Discover candidates with Resume Search in Recruiter.” https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a770588
- Greenhouse Support, “Talent Matching FAQ,” updated 2026-03-18. https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/41131886674075-Talent-Matching-FAQ

A 2026 paired-conjoint experiment with 1,700 recruiters in the United Kingdom and United States found that stated AI skills increased interview-invitation probabilities by approximately 8–15 percentage points across the studied office-assistant, software-engineer, and graphic-designer profiles. Effects differed by occupation and were weaker for graphic designers. Treat this as causal evidence inside the experiment's synthetic-profile setting, not a guarantee for a real vacancy or a reason to add unsupported AI claims.

Source: Fabian Stephany, Ole Teutloff, and Angelo Leone, “AI Skills Improve Job Prospects: Causal Evidence from a Hiring Experiment,” 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13286

The GMAC 2026 Corporate Recruiters Survey found that, among employers already concerned with AI skills, automating tasks was the most commonly valued specific application, followed by research and strategy/decision support. Its graduate-management-education population limits generalization, but it supports describing the work performed with AI instead of listing only a tool name.

Source: Graduate Management Admission Council, *Corporate Recruiters Survey 2026 Report*, published 2026-07. https://www.gmac.com/-/media/files/gmac/research/employment-outlook/2026-corporate-recruiters-survey/report.pdf

Recent employer research also reports an authenticity problem: AI-tailored application volume increases verification work, and hiring managers report difficulty determining whether claimed skills are real. The method therefore pairs AI-readable qualification clarity with inspectable proof and human accountability.

Source: Robert Half survey of more than 2,000 U.S. hiring managers, fielded 2025-11 and reported 2026-04. https://www.eminfo.com/pdf/205.pdf

## Practitioner and community layer

Official guidance is necessary for privacy, document requirements, and conservative defaults, but it does not fully explain why one truthful application feels compelling and another feels generic. The skill therefore also uses named hiring-manager essays, portfolio reviews, live portfolio examples, Medium articles, and Reddit discussions.

Those sources are stored and operationalized in `portfolio-and-practitioner-patterns.md`. They may support:

- review questions;
- curation and information-architecture choices;
- role-specific proof strategies;
- warnings about recurring failure modes;
- hypotheses to test against the vacancy, candidate, and rendered artifact.

They may not override employer instructions, privacy rules, verified evidence, or platform constraints. Anecdotes cannot establish an exact recruiter review time, a universal page limit, a guaranteed callback tactic, or a country's single preferred aesthetic.

## Claims deliberately excluded

Do not embed these common claims as rules without stronger context-specific evidence:

- “Recruiters spend exactly six or seven seconds on every CV.”
- “A specific percentage of CVs is automatically rejected by ATS.”
- “Repeating a keyword a particular number of times improves ranking.”
- “Every CV must be one page.”
- “Every European CV should contain a photo.”
- “Every Polish CV needs a RODO clause.”
- “Europass is preferred by all European employers.”
- “An ATS score from a third-party checker predicts interview probability.”
- “Every bullet needs a numeric result.”
- “AI recruiting systems use only exact keyword matching.”
- “Adding every synonym increases an AI match score.”
- “An AI-text detector can reliably determine who wrote a CV.”
- “Naming Codex proves AI proficiency.”
- “Using AI to draft a CV should be hidden from the employer.”

## Maintenance

When updating this method:

1. Prefer current official or primary sources.
2. Record publication/update and retrieval dates.
3. Separate empirical findings, platform behavior, law, conventions, and practitioner advice.
4. State population and market limitations.
5. Remove obsolete advice instead of accumulating contradictory rules.
6. Update `market-conventions.md`, `validation.md`, and this reference together when a source changes a rule.
7. Re-check AI recruiting platforms, candidate-facing AI rights, and official Codex documentation at least every 90 days, and immediately when the user requests `latest`, `current`, or `recent` guidance.
