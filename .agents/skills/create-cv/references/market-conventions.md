## Selection order

Apply conventions in this order:

1. Explicit employer or application-platform instructions.
2. The market and language of the specific role.
3. The candidate's confirmed preferences.
4. The conservative defaults below.

Research the current convention when a choice could affect eligibility, privacy, or a materially different dossier. Record the source and retrieval date. Do not generalize one country's convention to another.

## Universal privacy default

Include only information needed to identify, contact, assess, or establish the eligibility of the candidate.

Default to omitting:

- photograph;
- full street address;
- date or place of birth;
- age;
- marital or family status;
- gender;
- national identification numbers;
- nationality unless work eligibility makes it useful;
- references' contact details without their permission;
- blanket consent language not required by the employer or law.

Country custom can justify offering an optional variant, but never silently add sensitive personal data. Ask the user before including it.

## International English

- Use the language and spelling convention of the vacancy.
- Default to one page for early-career candidates and one or two pages for experienced candidates. Use more only when the role genuinely requires extensive evidence.
- Default to no photo and no sensitive personal details.
- Include city/region and country rather than a full street address unless requested.
- State work authorization or relocation intent only when relevant and verified.
- Use reverse chronology, standard section names, and a single-column ATS-safe layout.

Treat this as a portable default, not a claim about every English-speaking market.

## United Kingdom

- Call the document a `CV` unless the employer uses another term.
- Use British English unless the vacancy or employer uses another variety.
- Prefer two pages for an experienced professional; use one page when the evidence fits naturally. Do not exceed two pages without a role-specific reason or instruction.
- Include name, phone, professional email, a useful professional-profile link, and a broad location. Do not include age, date of birth, marital/civil-partnership status, nationality, a photograph, or a full street address by default.
- State verified UK work authorization or sponsorship needs only when it helps answer an explicit application question or removes material ambiguity. Do not include identity-document details or a right-to-work share code in the CV.
- Use a short role-specific profile only when it adds orientation. Keep work history reverse chronological and use concrete evidence.
- Do not publish a referee's contact details in the CV. Use `References available on request` only when the employer expects a references section; otherwise omit it.
- Follow anonymized-CV instructions literally. Some UK public-sector processes require removing the name, contact details, nationality, institutions, or other identifiers.

## United States

- Call the document a `resume` or `résumé` unless the employer explicitly requests a CV.
- Use American English and US Letter page assumptions unless the platform or employer specifies otherwise.
- Prefer one page for early-career candidates and one or two pages for experienced professionals. Use a second page when it adds relevant evidence; do not compress readability to force one page.
- Include name, phone, professional email, a useful professional-profile or portfolio link, and city/state or broader location. Do not include a photograph, age/date of birth, sex/gender, marital/family status, religion, Social Security number, health/disability information, or other protected personal details.
- Do not state citizenship by default. If the role raises an explicit eligibility or sponsorship question, state only the narrow verified work-authorization fact needed to answer it; never include document numbers.
- Use reverse chronology or a truthful combination format with a complete work history. Avoid functional formats that obscure dates or parse poorly.
- Convert qualifications into terms a US reader can understand without claiming false equivalence. Preserve the official qualification and institution name; add a concise verified clarification when useful.
- Treat US federal applications as a separate professional-resume variant. Read the full USAJOBS announcement and its `Qualifications`, `How You Will Be Evaluated`, and `Required Documents` sections. Current USAJOBS guidance limits ordinary federal resumes to two pages and requires explicit month/year dates, hours per week, and relevant qualification evidence. Follow an announcement-specific exception when it requests a long-form clinical or research CV.

## Poland

- Use the vacancy language. A foreign-language advertisement normally signals an application in that language unless instructed otherwise.
- Prefer one or two pages for a professional CV.
- Default to no photo. Polish law does not require a candidate to provide one.
- Do not add the traditional general RODO/GDPR consent clause automatically. Current UODO guidance states that consent is not needed to process the ordinary recruitment data identified in the Labour Code and calls the old blanket-clause practice inappropriate.
- Add consent wording only when a specific employer requests it, when the candidate intentionally provides additional data requiring consent, or for a clearly defined future-recruitment purpose. Use the employer's current wording where supplied; do not improvise legal text.
- Minimize personal data. Treat birth date as information an employer may lawfully request under Polish rules, not as a mandatory CV field.
- Use Polish diacritics and natural Polish role terminology. Preserve official foreign job titles when translation could change meaning; optionally add a clear Polish equivalent.
- In Polish narrative prose, prefer natural Polish equivalents such as `projektowanie produktów`, `system projektowy`, `testy użyteczności`, and `model samoobsługowy`. Retain English only for official titles, product names, exact qualification names, necessary vacancy terminology, or concepts without a clear established Polish equivalent. Reject mechanically inflected hybrids such as `designu`, `product designu`, or similarly literal English-to-Polish constructions.

If legal treatment is material or unclear, verify current UODO or statutory guidance and describe the result as informational, not legal advice.

## Norway

- Use the vacancy language unless the employer accepts or requests English.
- Tailor the summary (`sammendrag` or `nøkkelkvalifikasjoner`) to the role with concrete evidence.
- Use reverse chronology for employment and education.
- Include relevant authorizations, trade certificates, courses, driving licence, volunteering, and language levels when they matter to the role.
- State spoken and written language levels accurately; include the first language when useful.
- Default to one or two pages for an ordinary professional CV unless the employer or sector expects more.
- NAV examples include name, birth date, email, phone, and address, but use the universal privacy default for generated CVs. Offer birth date or fuller address only after user confirmation or a clear employer requirement.
- A photo is optional rather than required. Default to no photo, especially for ATS submission; add a professional photo only by explicit choice.
- Norwegian employers may value professional qualifications, teamwork, independence, and motivation. Demonstrate these through verified examples rather than unsupported trait lists.

## Switzerland

- Use the vacancy language. Prefer German, French, or Italian for locally oriented employers; international employers may accept English.
- A conventional Swiss application may be a dossier containing a motivation letter, CV, employment references, diplomas, and certificates. Include only the documents the employer or platform requests.
- Official Swiss guidance allows up to three CV pages. Prefer two pages for most experienced professional candidates; use a third page only when relevant evidence or the role's dossier convention warrants it.
- Use a complete reverse chronology. Explain material career changes or gaps only with verified, concise context, often in the cover letter rather than by inventing a CV narrative.
- Traditional Swiss guidance may include a photo, birth date, marital/family status, nationality, place of origin, and permit status. Do not add these automatically. Offer a traditional-local variant only after explicit user approval.
- A professional photo is optional. Default to no photo for ATS-heavy or international applications.
- Include nationality or Swiss residence/work permit only when it materially clarifies verified eligibility.
- References or “available on request” may be customary. Prefer the latter or a separate reference sheet; never disclose a referee's details without permission.
- Use Swiss orthography and terminology when writing German for Switzerland.

## Europass

Use Europass when:

- the employer, public institution, mobility program, education provider, or user requests it;
- cross-border comparability is more important than a custom professional layout; or
- the application channel is built around Europass/EURES.

Otherwise use the standard role-tailored CV. Europass's profile-and-selection model is useful: maintain comprehensive evidence centrally, then select only the facts relevant to the target. Do not assume its template is preferred by every European employer.
