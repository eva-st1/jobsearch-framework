## Outcome and inputs

Produce a role-specific professional CV that makes the strongest verified evidence easy to find, understand, and validate.

Require these inputs before finalization:

- complete job snapshot;
- company and role context;
- application language and target market;
- verified career facts and any exact imported profile snapshot;
- profile preferences and excluded fact IDs;
- renderer content contract, if rendering;
- employer instructions about format or dossier contents.

Drafting may begin with incomplete inputs, but label the result provisional and do not create a final artifact until factual blockers are resolved.

## Vacancy decomposition

Build a requirement matrix before writing prose. Do not copy the advertisement's self-description or marketing language into the CV.

For each requirement record:

- `requirement`: normalized employer need;
- `sourceText`: exact or tightly paraphrased vacancy wording;
- `class`: eligibility, core delivery, domain, tool/method, collaboration, leadership, language, location/work authorization, or differentiator;
- `priority`: must, strong, supporting, or incidental;
- `evidenceExpected`: what would credibly demonstrate the requirement;
- `verifiedEvidence`: supporting fact or snapshot identifiers;
- `coverage`: strong, partial, absent, or unknown;
- `risk`: ambiguity, adjacent experience, outdated evidence, or none.

Infer priority from explicit labels, repetition, placement, role outcomes, and company context. Keep inference separate from source fact.

Treat a must-have without verified support as a gap. Do not disguise it with synonyms.

## Evidence mapping

Use the smallest set of facts that proves the most important requirements.

Prefer evidence in this order:

1. Directly relevant, recent, observable outcomes.
2. Directly relevant scope, responsibility, or delivered output.
3. Transferable evidence with a clear and honest bridge.
4. Qualifications, certifications, education, or portfolio evidence.
5. Unsupported self-description only as a last resort—and omit it from the final CV unless it is itself verified and useful.

For every proposed line, be able to answer:

- Which source supports it?
- Does the source support every modifier, number, tool, and implied level of ownership?
- Is the relationship individual, shared, supported, led, or observed?
- Would the candidate defend the wording in an interview?

Preserve meaningful uncertainty. Use “contributed to,” “supported,” or precise task language when that is what the evidence supports.

## Application-channel map

Assume recruiters may evaluate a joined candidate packet rather than the CV alone. Map the information supplied through:

- CV or resume;
- LinkedIn or another professional profile;
- application-form fields and screening answers;
- cover letter or supporting statement;
- portfolio, GitHub, publications, or work samples;
- assessments and interview evidence.

For every must or strong requirement, record the canonical qualification, the vacancy's wording, the strongest verified evidence, where it appears, its date or recency, and any contradiction across channels.

Keep official job titles and employers accurate. When an official title is ambiguous to the target market, add a verified functional descriptor rather than replacing the title. Name skills in recognizable current terms, then demonstrate them in context. Do not repeat synonyms merely to create more matches.

Read `ai-recruiting-and-codex-evidence.md` for current AI-assisted sourcing and evaluation behavior, cross-channel consistency, and the Codex evidence ladder.

## Positioning

Write a one-sentence internal thesis:

> Candidate type + strongest relevant capability + employer problem + differentiating verified evidence.

Do not place this formula verbatim in the CV. Use it to control selection and order.

Choose three to five evidence themes. Each must:

- answer a high-priority requirement;
- have at least one strong verified example;
- be distinct enough to deserve space;
- remain consistent across summary, experience, and skills.

The top third of page one should establish identity, target relevance, and strongest proof without requiring the reader to infer the connection.

## Section construction

### Header

Include the candidate's name and direct contact routes as ordinary document text. Add location, work authorization, professional links, or portfolio only when useful and verified. Follow market-specific rules for personal data.

Never place essential contact information only in a page header, footer, graphic, or text box.

### Professional headline

Use a recognizable role family or defensible specialization. The headline may align with the target title only when verified experience supports the equivalence. Do not award the candidate a seniority level they have not held or demonstrated.

### Summary

Use a summary only when it improves orientation or explains a non-obvious fit. Unless the candidate records another preference, use one concise headline followed by one continuous body paragraph of roughly 35–60 words. The headline should use the same content width as the body copy. Do not isolate a short opening sentence as its own paragraph; shorten or rebalance the copy rather than reducing the font size. Use short bullets only when the employer-requested format or candidate preference clearly benefits from them.

Include concrete identity, relevant scope, domain, and differentiating evidence. Exclude objectives, generic enthusiasm, unsupported traits, third-person biography, and keyword lists disguised as prose.

### Experience

Use reverse chronological roles. Preserve employer, title, employment type, location, and dates exactly as supported.

For each role:

1. Add a one-line scope statement only when the employer or remit needs context.
2. Order bullets by relevance and evidence strength, not by chronology of tasks.
3. Prefer three to six bullets for the most relevant recent roles; compress older or less relevant roles.
4. Preserve continuity. Do not use a functional structure to conceal dates or gaps.

Compose a bullet from the supported parts of this model:

> Action or contribution + object/problem + relevant scope or method + observable output/result.

An outcome need not be numeric. A shipped product, adopted process, resolved constraint, completed migration, approved decision, or improved capability can be specific evidence when verified. Never invent a baseline, percentage, causality, or business impact.

### Skills

Group concrete, role-relevant skills into a few readable categories. Prefer recognized names used by the vacancy and market when they accurately describe verified evidence.

Do not:

- stuff variants or invisible keywords;
- list every technology ever encountered;
- use proficiency bars, stars, or unexplained ratings;
- list generic soft skills without evidence elsewhere;
- infer a skill merely because it is typical of an occupation.

ESCO may help normalize multilingual occupation and skill terminology, but it is a vocabulary aid, not evidence that the candidate possesses a skill.

Treat AI tools as methods, not self-authenticating skills. List `OpenAI Codex`, another named tool, or a general capability such as `AI-assisted software delivery` only when verified. Prefer a contextual project or experience line over a naked tool name when the vacancy materially values AI fluency.

### Education, certifications, and languages

Use the official qualification name, institution, location, and dates. Add grade, thesis, coursework, or awards only when relevant and verified.

For languages, state the verified level using the candidate's existing scale or a recognized framework such as CEFR. Do not translate conversational ability into “professional fluency.”

List current and relevant certifications with issuer and date or validity where material. Do not imply an expired credential is current.

### Optional sections

Include projects, publications, patents, volunteering, board roles, awards, speaking, courses, or interests only when they add relevant evidence, explain the profile, or follow a market convention. Apply the same verification rules as employment.

## Proof architecture

Decide whether the application needs only a CV or a two-layer evidence system:

- `CV only`: sufficient for roles where a concise employment record is the expected proof and no relevant public work sample exists.
- `CV + proof links`: useful when one or two artifacts can verify a material capability without requiring a full portfolio.
- `CV + portfolio`: expected or advantageous for design, creative, writing, research, engineering, product, data, marketing, consulting, and other roles where actual work can be inspected.
- `CV + private case-study deck`: use when work is confidential and the candidate has permission to share a sanitized account in a controlled interview setting.

The CV must still stand alone. A reviewer should understand the candidate's role, relevance, and supported outcomes without opening a link. The portfolio must add evidence rather than repeat the CV in a larger format.

For every portfolio or work-sample link, record:

- the requirement or evidence theme it supports;
- the candidate's exact role and contribution;
- the verified facts used in its title, teaser, and outcome;
- whether the artifact is public, permissioned, sanitized, or private;
- whether the link is live, mobile-readable, and accessible without an unnecessary sign-in;
- whether the destination still matches the wording in the CV.

Use meaningful visible labels such as `Selected product case studies`, `GitHub: deployment tooling`, or `Writing sample: policy analysis`. Avoid raw link dumps, QR codes as the only route, or vague labels such as `click here`.

Read `portfolio-and-practitioner-patterns.md` for the evidence structure, role-specific proof menu, and practitioner-derived quality checks.

## Writing rules

- Write in the language of the vacancy unless the employer or user specifies otherwise.
- Use natural professional language for that market, not literal translation.
- Prefer precise nouns and verbs over adjectives and claims of excellence.
- Use consistent tense: present for ongoing work, past for completed work.
- Expand uncommon abbreviations on first use. Keep standard industry abbreviations when they improve recognition.
- Use numerals consistently. Preserve the source's precision.
- Vary sentence openings only when clarity permits; do not force a thesaurus.
- Avoid personal pronouns unless the target convention requires them.
- Avoid clichés such as “results-driven,” “dynamic,” “passionate,” or “team player” unless replaced by evidence.
- Proofread names, dates, diacritics, terminology, grammar, and punctuation in the target language.

## Compression and page strategy

Page limits are design constraints, not targets. Evidence quality and the candidate's active instructions control the content; the renderer does not. First remove low-value content, and do not solve overflow by shrinking text below comfortable reading size or by weakening the roles that carry the application.

Before compressing, classify every section and evidence line as:

- `protected core evidence`: directly proves a must/strong requirement or is protected by candidate feedback;
- `useful secondary evidence`: relevant but not decisive;
- `duplicated orientation`: repeats evidence already clear elsewhere;
- `optional`: low-value for this vacancy.

For the strongest two or three roles, preserve the distinct problem, contribution, scope/method, and outcome/output evidence that the verified record supports. A short profile or focus card never substitutes for substantive experience evidence.

Compress in this order:

1. Remove unsupported claims.
2. Remove duplicated orientation, repeated toolkit labels, and repeated summaries.
3. Remove optional credentials, languages, testimonials, or secondary sections that do not affect fit.
4. Remove or shorten vacancy-irrelevant roles before cutting protected core evidence.
5. Merge genuinely overlapping bullets without merging distinct claims incorrectly.
6. Shorten context around protected evidence, not the evidence itself.
7. Adjust spacing conservatively while preserving readable type.
8. Allow another page when employer instructions permit it and further compression would weaken protected evidence.

Do not delete dates, employers, titles, distinct roles, candidate-required photographs, focus items, or essential context merely to force a page count. Do not collapse a role into `Earlier experience` to work around renderer pagination unless the candidate explicitly approves that exact tradeoff. Use the market-specific page guidance in `market-conventions.md`, subordinate to employer instructions and explicit candidate preferences.

After compression, compare the revision against the candidate-feedback ledger and the most evidence-rich verified artifact. Record every deletion and confirm that requirement coverage and the evidence density of protected roles did not decrease. If they did, revert the compression or obtain explicit candidate approval.

## Decision record

Record at least:

- positioning thesis and themes;
- requirement matrix or its structured summary;
- selected and omitted evidence with reasons;
- wording decisions for adjacent or partial evidence;
- job-language terms normalized or translated;
- market and personal-data choices;
- portfolio decision, selected proof, confidentiality treatment, and link-to-requirement mapping;
- page-limit and section-order choices;
- ATS/layout choices;
- application-channel consistency decisions and any disclosed AI-review process;
- AI/Codex evidence level, supporting artifacts, human-oversight boundary, and source recency;
- unresolved gaps and questions;
- candidate-feedback ledger with scope, source, status, and supersession;
- deletion ledger for every material removal from the comparison artifacts;
- protected roles/evidence and their before/after evidence density;
- candidate-approved exceptions for photograph, focus-item count, role consolidation, or reduced core-role evidence;
- `cvMethodology` with repository Git revision and `SKILL.md` SHA-256.

## Submission filename

Use an employer-ready download name in the form `<Candidate_Name>_CV_<Company>_<Role>.pdf`, transliterated to safe ASCII and without artifact IDs, database IDs, draft labels, or revision numbers. Apply the equivalent document label to other artifacts, such as `Cover_Letter` or `Application_Answer`.
