## Current platform signals

LinkedIn's current guidance favors real voices, authentic perspectives, and lived expertise. Its May 2026 newsroom statement distinguishes helpful AI-assisted refinement from low-effort, generic AI content that lacks unique perspective or substance. Treat author contribution and voice fidelity as publishing requirements, not cosmetic polish.

LinkedIn's official sharing guide recommends articles that share insights, perspectives, and expertise; timely commentary, strong headlines, cover images, and roughly 500–1,000 words. Treat this length as a platform default, not a target that overrides the idea.

LinkedIn's thought-leadership guidance emphasizes accuracy, relevance, digestibility, shareability, and actionability. Its synthesis of buyer research highlights strong data, clearer understanding of business challenges, concrete guidance, and case studies as signals of quality.

LinkedIn distinguishes short feed posts from long-form articles. Newsletters can build a recurring audience because subscribers receive notifications for new editions. Recommend a newsletter only when the author has a coherent topic and realistic cadence; do not turn a single article into an ongoing commitment by default.

## Practical synthesis

Apply these defaults unless fresher primary guidance contradicts them:

- Lead with a specific, supportable point of view rather than a generic trend recap.
- Use personal experience only when verified and relevant; lived detail is evidence, not decoration.
- Make research serve the reader's decision or understanding.
- Optimize for trust and usefulness rather than speculative algorithm tricks.
- Use a clear headline, readable subheads, short paragraphs, and a relevant cover-image concept.
- End with one proportionate next step; avoid engagement bait.
- Keep AI assistance subordinate to the author's thinking, judgment, and language.
- Review for templated constructions that make unrelated authors sound identical.

Do not make universal claims about reach, best posting time, hashtag count, or algorithmic rewards without current first-party evidence. Label practitioner observations as observations, not platform facts.

## AIDA basis

AIDA is the sequence Attention, Interest, Desire, and Action. Historical research shows that its familiar formulation emerged through early advertising and selling literature rather than from one clean, uncontested invention. Use it as a durable planning heuristic, not a scientifically complete model of reader behavior.

For LinkedIn thought leadership:

- translate **Attention** into a truthful headline and opening;
- translate **Interest** into relevance, context, and credible development;
- translate **Desire** into the reader wanting a better professional state or clearer understanding;
- translate **Action** into a useful, low-friction next step.

The framework does not prove that a draft will perform well. Evaluate truth, novelty, audience fit, and voice separately.

## Refresh protocol

Before giving platform-specific advice or drafting around a “hot” topic:

1. Search LinkedIn Help, LinkedIn Newsroom, and LinkedIn Marketing Solutions for newer guidance.
2. Verify both publication date and the date of the underlying event or product change.
3. Prefer current primary sources; use credible practitioner evidence only to fill gaps.
4. Record URLs and retrieval date in working notes.
5. Update this reference only when the repository change is in scope; otherwise state that fresher guidance superseded the baseline.
